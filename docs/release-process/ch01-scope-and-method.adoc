= Scope, vocabulary and method
:description: What this specification covers, how statements are classified, which chapters are normative, and the conventions this book follows.

*Scope:* What the framework covers, how statements are classified, which parts of the book are normative and the documentation conventions it follows.

*Status:* Proposal for review. Nothing in this book is authoritative until the CTO accepts it and a reviewed pull request adopts it.

== Scope

The framework governs how a reviewed change in `CadenaBitcoin/signer_app` becomes a delivered, verified build of the Cadena Signer application on Android and iOS, in the environments *development*, *staging* and *production*.

It covers governance, branch promotion, versioning, build, authorization, signing, verification, delivery, evidence, recovery and the external infrastructure those depend on.

It does not cover application features, the Rust library's cryptographic design or the backend.

== Two things that must never be confused

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Term | Meaning

| *Pipeline implementation and verification*
| Building, testing and dry-running the delivery machinery for an environment, without releasing anything. Needs engineering review. Uses no production credential.

| *Release authorization*
| The CTO's written, version-bound decision that one specific build may be signed and delivered. Needs the authorization record, the environment approval and the credential for that delivery.
|===

Production pipeline work (xref:ch10-production-extensions.adoc#ch10[Chapter 10]) is implementation and verification only. It authorizes no production release.

== Classification used in this specification

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Class | Meaning

| *A. Decided and implemented*
| The decision or requirement is approved and the mechanism exists in the current system. Limits of the available verification are stated where they apply.

| *B. Decided, not yet fully reflected*
| The decision or requirement is approved, but the authoritative documentation, the current implementation or both do not yet reflect it. The current implementation remains authoritative until the reviewed implementation change takes effect.

| *C. Established, insufficiently enforced*
| A requirement exists in policy or in a decision; nothing, or only a person, enforces it.

| *D. Improvement*
| Implementation or operational work that does not change policy or architecture.

| *E. Temporary exception*
| A deviation accepted for a stated scope or until a stated condition. It stays an exception until a disposition is approved. A staging exception does not extend to production.

| *F. Genuinely unresolved*
| A question that the staging reference does not answer and that materially affects architecture, security or governance.
|===

Evidence labels from the audits are kept: VERIFIED, DOCUMENTED BUT NOT VERIFIED, INFERRED, UNKNOWN.

An approved decision does not imply that its implementation exists, and an implementation does not imply that its behavior has been verified.

== Normative and historical content

The following chapters state what is currently required or decided: xref:ch02-established-decisions.adoc#ch02[Chapter 2], xref:ch03-unresolved-decisions.adoc#ch03[Chapter 3], the sections of xref:ch04-exceptions-and-reconciliation.adoc#ch04[Chapter 4] on open exceptions, accepted limitations, reconciliation of historical documents and exception management, and Chapters 5 to 11.

The sections of Chapter 4 that form the historical record (superseded requirements, closed exceptions, retired identifiers and the staging recovery decisions) and the revision history in xref:ch12-revision-history.adoc#ch12[Chapter 12] are not normative. They keep the text, rationale and surviving security property of superseded requirements, so that reasoning is not lost.

A statement that a later decision replaced is removed from the normative chapters. Where the repository today differs from an approved target, the requirement states the target, the current mechanism is stated as such, and the as-built page (`STA-01`) and the transitional rules of xref:ch09-migration-sequence.adoc#ch09[Chapter 9] govern the interval.

A requirement is never described as implemented because it is documented. Every requirement states its enforcement type, and every mechanism that is not yet demonstrated is an invariant with the investigation that must demonstrate it (xref:ch11-build-and-release-architecture.adoc#ch11[Chapter 11]).

== Stable identifiers

An identifier is never reused or renumbered. A retired identifier is kept in the historical record of xref:ch04-exceptions-and-reconciliation.adoc#ch04[Chapter 4].

Each family has one defining chapter. Other chapters cite or summarize an identifier; they never redefine it.

[cols="2,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Identifier family | Defined in | Meaning

| `DEC-NN`
| xref:ch02-established-decisions.adoc#ch02[Chapter 2]
| Established decision.

| `UNR-NN`
| xref:ch03-unresolved-decisions.adoc#ch03[Chapter 3]
| Unresolved or deferred decision.

| `EXC-NN`, `REC-NN`, `SUP-NN`
| xref:ch04-exceptions-and-reconciliation.adoc#ch04[Chapter 4]
| Temporary exception or accepted limitation, reconciliation of a historical document, superseded requirement.

| `GOV`, `BRN`, `VER`, `CFG`, `AUT`, `RCV`, `CRD`, `BLD`, `SIG`, `DEL`, `EVD`, `DOC`, `INF` with `-NN`
| xref:ch05-requirements-and-enforcement.adoc#ch05[Chapter 5]
| Requirement.

| `PRD-NN`
| xref:ch10-production-extensions.adoc#ch10[Chapter 10]
| Production-specific requirement.

| `WP-xN`, `Wn`, `Pn`
| xref:ch09-migration-sequence.adoc#ch09[Chapter 9]
| Work package, controlled-change window, implementation step.

| `FQ-NN`, `INV-NN`, `I-NN`
| xref:ch11-build-and-release-architecture.adoc#ch11[Chapter 11]
| Feasibility question, architectural invariant, operational interpretation.

| `RUN-NN`, `STA-01`
| xref:ch06-document-hierarchy-and-mapping.adoc#ch06[Chapter 6]
| Names of future documentation pages (runbooks and the as-built page). They are not defined inside this book.
|===

Identifiers from outside this book keep their own meaning. The decision labels of the staging record (`S1` to `S4`, `C1` to `C4`, `R1` to `R3`) refer to `docs/release-records/ios-staging-authorization.md`. Finding identifiers of the Phase 1 audit (for example `REP-04`) refer to that audit. Where an audit identifier has the same form as a requirement identifier of this book, the identifier of this book is meant unless the text says "audit".

== Documentation conventions of this book

These are the conventions that `DOC-01` to `DOC-04` require of the future documentation tree. This book follows them.

. Every chapter is a standalone page. It starts with a chapter anchor (`+[[chNN]]+`), a title, a scope line and a status line, and it ends with a References block.
. Cross-references use anchors through `xref:` macros, never a bare file name. Requirement and decision identifiers are cited by identifier and located through the defining chapter named above.
. Each identifier is defined once. Summaries elsewhere cite it.
. Machine-read files keep their format; this book is human documentation in native AsciiDoc only.
. A chapter renders without its neighbors. It may cite another chapter by anchor but does not depend on that chapter's text to be understood within its own scope.
. Table cells that contain lists or delimited blocks use the native AsciiDoc `a` cell style.
. The master document (`index.adoc`) assembles the chapters with include directives and a level offset; chapters carry no attributes that the master must override.
